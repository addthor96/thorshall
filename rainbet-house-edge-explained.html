Rainbet House Edge Explained

House edge is the casino's mathematical advantage over a large number of wagers. RTP is the same idea expressed from the player's side.

House edge vs RTP

House edge ≈ 100% − RTP

If a game has a theoretical RTP of 96%, its theoretical house edge is about 4%. That does not mean every player loses 4% in one session. Short sessions can finish far above or below the theoretical average.

Why wager volume matters

The expected loss from a house edge grows with total wager volume:

Expected loss ≈ total wagered × house edge

Example: $10,000 of total wagers at a 4% theoretical house edge implies an expected loss of about $400 over a sufficiently large sample. Actual results can be much higher or lower because of variance.

Variance

Variance describes how widely short-term outcomes can move around the expected average. High-volatility slots or crash-style games can produce long losing stretches and occasional large wins. A lucky or unlucky streak does not change the underlying probability of future independent outcomes.

Can a betting system remove the edge?

No staking pattern changes the mathematical house edge of an independent casino game. Systems such as Martingale change the size and timing of bets, not the underlying probability. They can also create a risk of very large bets after a losing sequence.

Check the game's own RTP

RTP differs by game and sometimes by configuration. Use the rules or information panel for the specific game you are playing rather than assuming every game on Rainbet has the same RTP.
